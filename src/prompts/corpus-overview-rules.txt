This is an overview of a set of documents, not a summary of each and not a
summary of them laid end to end. The material is the set's condensed evidence,
document by document, followed by a relation plan prepared from it. Write the
overall picture: what the set is about, or that it is heterogeneous and what
its clusters and outliers are; the themes that recur; where documents agree,
disagree, repeat, qualify, elaborate, or correct one another, and what each
document contributes that the others do not; the chronology the documents
themselves support; and what the set leaves unresolved. Refer to a document
by the reference or short handle the evidence gives it, in ordinary prose,
never by an identifier such as D001 and never by a file name. Every document
appears in the Detailed reading. Do not infer order, authority, or importance
from the order the documents are listed in, and do not force a common thesis
on documents that do not share one. The Brief keeps the set's principal
structure, its strongest agreements and disagreements, and its most important
open questions.

Organize the Detailed reading by the set's questions, themes, and relations,
not by document. Do not give each document its own successive mini-summary.
Name a document when attributing a distinctive contribution, disagreement,
or qualification, but each body section should normally compare or connect
at least two documents. Reserve a single-document paragraph for a genuine
outlier or unique contribution, and explain how it changes the overall
picture.

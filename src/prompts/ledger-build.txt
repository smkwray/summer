UNIT BOUNDARY
A unit is the smallest independently omittable proposition, or a tightly coupled
claim-support-qualification packet, that a careful reader needs in order to understand this
section. Create separate units when the source separately presents any of the following:
- a question, thesis, definition, assumption, or scope condition;
- a mechanism or a distinct step in a mechanism;
- a method, identification choice, data construction, or validation test;
- evidence, a result, a direction, or a quantity that changes interpretation;
- a scenario, comparison, alternative explanation, limitation, uncertainty, or implication.

Do not collapse an entire section into a theme. Two propositions belong in one unit only when
neither can be omitted without making the other misleading or incomplete. Do not split one
proposition merely to create more units. Repeated statements inside this section may share one
unit. Do not suppress a proposition because it may repeat an abstract, introduction, or conclusion
elsewhere; cross-section reconciliation happens later.

ACCOUNTING
Every source block must be covered in exactly one of these ways:
1. assigned to one or more units; or
2. given one narrow disposition:
   - exact_repetition: the same proposition is already represented by a named unit in this section;
   - apparatus: citation-only material, bibliography, page furniture, or raw table syntax with no
     unique substantive fact;
   - incidental_example: an example that adds no distinct mechanism, result, qualification, or
     implication;
   - source_only_detail: implementation detail that can be removed without changing any claim,
     support, quantity, definition, condition, limitation, or conclusion at Detailed depth.

A block containing both apparatus and substantive content must be assigned to a unit. Never use a
Brief-scope judgment to omit material from Detailed. For exact_repetition, name the represented
unit's local_id. For every other disposition, give a concrete reason tied to the block.

DEPTH
Decide dispositions per unit, not per source block.
- Detailed is a comprehensive condensation. Mark a unit required when omitting it would leave a
  substantive section, mechanism, method, result, comparison, scenario, validation step, or
  material qualification unexplained. Mark optional only when it improves understanding but is not
  needed for that comprehensive account. Mark omit only for genuinely nonessential detail.
- Brief is a shorter but still explanatory reading artifact. It must include the central question,
  thesis, principal mechanisms, headline evidence/results, decisive quantities, and every
  qualification needed to interpret an included claim. Mark brief_priority from 1 (indispensable)
  through 5 (peripheral). A unit may depend on another unit; list those dependencies so selection
  cannot create dangling references.

CAPSULES
Each capsule must be independently usable by a later composer and must preserve attribution,
certainty, direction, quantities, definitions, conditions, alternatives, and required qualifiers.
The capsules ARE the product and the only record of the unit: there is no separate claim,
support, qualifier or anchor field to fall back on, so a qualification that does not appear in a
capsule is lost.
- detailed_capsule: 40-110 words and 2-5 sentences; a typical unit warrants about
  80 words. Use fewer only for a genuinely atomic definition or result. Use fewer words only for a genuinely
  atomic definition or result. Use more only when a necessary qualification or multi-step mechanism
  cannot otherwise remain intact.
- brief_capsule: 25-75 words and 1-3 self-contained sentences, typically about 55 words, or an empty string only when
  brief_disposition is omit. It may omit supporting detail, but it may not turn an explained claim
  into a bare assertion or drop a qualification attached to an included claim.

VOICE
Use impersonal or third-person summary voice. Never imitate the source's first person: no "I",
"we", "my", or "our" as the summary narrator. Do not include citations, YAML, comments,
bibliography, task narration, audit language, or raw table rows.

Write CONTINUOUS PROSE. No bullet points, no numbered lists, no nested lists, no tables, no
markdown headings inside a capsule. These are read on an e-ink device and aloud by a
text-to-speech voice, where a list reads as a stack of fragments and a table is unreadable. When
the source presents a list, render it as a sentence that names the items and says what they have
in common.
